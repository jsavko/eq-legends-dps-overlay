Boss timers — their own window

Three ways to give the learned recast countdowns a home of their own, at 1:1 scale in the shipping palette. All three assume the same underlying change: timers leave the alert stack, get their own draggable window, their own placement and their own toggle.

Today — why it doesn't work

Timers sit at the bottom of the alert window's single vertical flow. Every warning that arrives or expires above them moves them (524 displacement events across the live log), and a timer whose cast fires vanishes while its warning is up, then returns (10,525 samples hidden that way; 72 re-arms within a minute; median chip life 14s, with 67 of 129 runs under 15 seconds). You cannot read a countdown that keeps moving and blinking.

A — Buff-window slots (recommended)

A framed panel with fixed slots, closest to EQ's buff window. A slot is claimed by a (boss, ability) pair the first time it arms and holds that row for the whole fight: when the cast fires the slot says CAST and re-arms in place; when a rhythm retracts the slot dims instead of collapsing. Nothing ever reorders, so a row you learned to glance at stays where you learned it. The row is the bar, the same idea the meter is built on. When the fight ends the panel vanishes — no empty frame, no placeholder rows; the only time it shows empty is while unlocked, because a window with nothing in it cannot be dragged.

B — Caster sections

Entity-first, like the boss window: each named mob gets a header and its abilities sit beneath it as bars. Reads well with two bosses up at once and makes "who is this timer for" unmissable — at the cost of a header row per caster, and the panel's height changing as casters engage and die.

C — Minimal ticker

No frame, no header: just bars over the game, the nearest one enlarged as the lead. Cheapest to build and the least screen it can possibly take — but it is invisible when empty (nothing to drag), it drops the caster name at a glance, and being sorted by what's next it reorders exactly as often as today's version does.

Where it sits

The alert window owns top-centre because a banner has to cross your eyeline. A timer panel is a fixture you consult, not a thing that must interrupt you — so it defaults beside the meter, roughly where EQ players keep the buff window, and can be dragged anywhere.
